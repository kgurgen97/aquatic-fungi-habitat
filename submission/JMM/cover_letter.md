Please consider our manuscript, “Sequencing depth changes which taxa meet metabarcoding evidence thresholds: implications for habitat-preference analysis”, as a Research Article in Journal of Microbiological Methods.

We studied how minimum-evidence thresholds affect habitat-preference analyses of metabarcoding data. Using FunAqua and NEON, we combined paired observations, a controlled sequencing-depth experiment, simulations and an estimator benchmark. In NEON, testable fungal orders at 100 and 10,000 reads shared only 28% of their union, although the median apparent habitat-preference effect changed by 0.024. Between datasets, 26 of 69 shared testable orders differed after correction for multiple tests. These comparisons also have limitations, which we describe in the paper.

The work addresses an analytical problem relevant to microbial ecology and high-throughput sequencing: two surveys can report similar aggregate effects while testing different taxa. We describe when this happens and why sequencing depth, taxonomic resolution and minimum-evidence rules should be documented when studies are compared.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration by another journal. The data sources and code repository are described in the manuscript: https://github.com/kgurgen97/aquatic-fungi-habitat.
